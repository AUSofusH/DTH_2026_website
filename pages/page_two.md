## The disadvantages of open source software: 

### Liability: 

While the MIT License is great from protecting developers by limiting liability, allowing them to work on their project without fear of litigation and legal disputes, this can also reduce the potential attractiveness of adoption by large organizations. The MIT license explicitly states: "The software is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind" and "in no event shall the authors or copyright holders be liable for any claim, damages or other liability" (Open Source Initiative, n.d.). This means if a company was to use an open-source MIT licensed software in their operations, and said software suddenly stopped functioning, the company would have no legal recourse against the developers. The risk of lost revenue from broken software could very well dissuade larger, less flexible organizations from using open source products.  

### Reliance on volunteerism: 

Another downside of the MIT license, and open source projects in general is its reliance on volunteers and passion as the main drivers for software development. While commercial software providers are contractually bound by some form of Service Level Agreement (SLA) to maintain their software's functionality for its users (Goodwin, 2024), MIT licensed open-source projects are not required to do anything. For example, a major outage of Microsoft 365 services occurred on November 13th, preventing users from utilizing services they have paid for (Ho-Him & Chan, 2026). Microsoft scrambled to fix the problem, but even if they quickly restored access to services, their SLA requires them to compensate customers affected by software downtime (Microsoft, n.d.). Had this been an open source service, affected users would have no legal recourse for potential lost income and access and developers are not obligated to restore functionality or fix the underlying issue within a specific timeframe. If for instance a developer decides to go on hiatus, users of the software would require a skilled volunteer to fork the software and continue development, to receive future updates.
